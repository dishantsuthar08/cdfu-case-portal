Subject: Suspected unauthorized access to confidential corporate data through a third‑party cloud application.

Respected Sir/Madam,

I am writing in my capacity as Chief Information Security Officer of Vantage Meridian Technologies to formally report a suspected data‑security incident affecting confidential corporate information held in our cloud environment.

On 02 June 2025, members of our Corporate Development team identified that internal, confidential deal documents — maintained under the internal codename “Project Lighthouse” — had appeared on an external file‑sharing location outside any of our sanctioned systems. These documents are classified Restricted and are accessible to only a small number of employees.

Our internal review to date shows no malware, no ransomware, and no forced entry to our network. Instead, the confidential files appear to have been reached through a third‑party productivity application that was authorized against a genuine employee account. The employee in question, a Senior Analyst in Corporate Development, states that she does not recall knowingly granting the access that our logs describe, yet our identity provider records a successful, multi‑factor‑verified authorization.

Because the activity resembles ordinary, authorized business use, our team has been unable to determine, with confidence, whether a genuine account was compromised, whether an employee acted deliberately, whether one of our technology vendors was involved, or some combination of these. We therefore request a formal forensic investigation.

We have preserved the relevant identity logs, cloud audit records, application authorization records and endpoint images, and will make our IT, SOC, HR and Legal personnel available. I affirm that the statements above are true to the best of my knowledge.

Initial Incident Report

What is known, and what is not, at the opening of the inquiry

What was discovered

On 02 June 2025, a Corp‑Dev analyst recognized several Project Lighthouse documents on an external file‑sharing page while researching the acquisition target. The CISO confirmed the files matched internal Restricted copies and initiated a review. The review found no malware and no interactive intruder logins, but did find that a Cadence‑branded application held broad, standing access to the affected mailbox and drive.

How it appears to have happened (preliminary)

The access path runs through an application authorization, not a password breach. A third‑party app was granted permission against Nadia Okafor's account on 30 May 2025. The permission included the ability to read files and mail and to continue doing so without the user being present (offline access). Data access then occurred as application activity over the following days, while the user's own sign‑in records show her offline.

KNOWN FACTS
  • Restricted Lighthouse files appeared externally on 02 Jun.
  • A Cadence‑branded app holds broad access to Nadia's data.
  • An MFA‑verified authorization exists dated 30 May 16:12.
  • No malware / ransomware / brute force detected.
  • File access occurred while the user was offline.
UNKNOWN FACTS
  • Whether the 30 May app is the same app used since April.
  • Whether Nadia intended to grant what was granted.
  • Who registered / controls the application.
  • Whether any employee or vendor assisted.
  • Where the data ultimately went, and who benefits.
