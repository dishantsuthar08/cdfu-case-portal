What Actually Happened

Solvex Analytics was defrauded by an external, financially motivated attacker — not by an insider acting maliciously. The fraud succeeded because two small, unconnected human mistakes gave the attacker everything they needed.

1. The access: Vikram Rao, a remote IT contractor, was spear-phished with a fake freelance job offer from "Meridian Talent Partners" — a shell identity registered on the domain meridian-systerns.com. The attached "project brief" harvested his VPN credentials. The attacker used them, off-hours, to browse the finance shared drive and a legacy mail-sync rule Vikram had set up months earlier — no password ever needed.

2. The internal detail: Separately, Devraj Solanki, under financial pressure, was contacted by a fake "market researcher" and shared a screenshot of the approved-vendor list for a small payment — never realizing it would be used for fraud.

3. The fraud: Combining both, the attacker emailed Ananya Kapoor from accounts@meridian-systerns.com, referencing the real invoice number, requesting updated bank details. Because it was treated as an existing-vendor update, no verbal confirmation was required — and the payment went through.

Verdict: No one at Solvex acted with criminal intent. Vikram Rao and Devraj Solanki were each negligent, not complicit. Farah Sheikh's finance-floor visits were unrelated — a statutory compliance audit, confirmed by timestamps that fall entirely outside the fraud window. Ananya Kapoor followed process exactly as written; the process itself had a gap, since closed by Solvex Finance.

Recommendation on file: Verbal confirmation now required for any bank-detail change, existing vendor or not. Legacy mail-sync rules audited and disabled cell-wide.
